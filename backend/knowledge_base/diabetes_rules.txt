DIABETES KNOWLEDGE BASE

1. HIGH GLUCOSE AFTER MEALS

High glucose after meals may happen when a meal contains high carbohydrates such as rice, sweets, sugary drinks, bread, potatoes, fried snacks, or processed foods.

For mild high glucose without serious symptoms, safe lifestyle actions may include drinking water, avoiding additional sugary food, taking a light 10–15 minute walk if the person feels well, and monitoring glucose again as advised by their healthcare provider.

A person should contact a healthcare professional if glucose remains very high, if high readings happen repeatedly, or if symptoms such as vomiting, confusion, severe dizziness, chest pain, breathing difficulty, or extreme weakness occur.

2. LOW GLUCOSE SAFETY

Low glucose may be dangerous. Symptoms can include sweating, shaking, confusion, weakness, hunger, dizziness, blurred vision, fast heartbeat, or fainting.

If the person has severe symptoms, confusion, unconsciousness, seizure, or cannot safely eat or drink, urgent medical help is required.

The chatbot should not give exact treatment dosage unless it is part of a doctor-approved care plan.

3. FOOD GUIDANCE

Food choices that may support glucose control include vegetables, pulses, lentils, beans, curd, paneer, eggs, lean protein, nuts in controlled quantity, and fiber-rich meals.

Meals with high refined carbohydrates may raise glucose quickly. Examples include white rice, sweets, sugary drinks, white bread, biscuits, cakes, packaged juices, and fried snacks.

A balanced plate may include:
- half plate non-starchy vegetables
- one quarter protein
- one quarter controlled carbohydrates

4. INDIAN MEAL CONTEXT

Common Indian meals like rice, roti, poha, upma, dosa, idli, paratha, and sweets can affect glucose depending on portion size, preparation method, and combination with protein or fiber.

If glucose is already high, the person may choose lighter meals, smaller portions of carbohydrates, more vegetables, and avoid sweets or sugary drinks.

5. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY

Light walking after meals may help reduce post-meal glucose for some people.

The person should avoid exercise if they feel dizzy, weak, have chest pain, breathing difficulty, vomiting, or have been advised by a doctor not to exercise.

Physical activity advice should be general and safe, not intense or risky.

6. HYDRATION

Drinking water may help prevent dehydration when glucose is high.

Sugary drinks, packaged juices, soda, and sweet tea or coffee may increase glucose and should be avoided during high glucose episodes.

7. MEDICATION SAFETY

The chatbot must not prescribe medicine, change insulin dosage, recommend stopping medication, or tell the patient to take extra medicine.

Medication changes should only be done by a qualified healthcare professional.

The chatbot may remind the user to follow their doctor’s prescribed plan.

8. EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS

Emergency warning signs include:
- chest pain
- breathing difficulty
- confusion
- fainting
- unconsciousness
- seizure
- repeated vomiting
- severe weakness
- very high glucose with symptoms
- very low glucose with symptoms

If these are present, the chatbot should recommend urgent medical help.

9. WHEN TO CONTACT A DOCTOR

The user should contact a healthcare provider if:
- glucose remains high repeatedly
- glucose drops frequently
- symptoms become worse
- new symptoms appear
- they are unsure about medication
- they feel unwell after eating or exercising

10. CHATBOT LIMITATIONS

This chatbot provides educational and lifestyle guidance only.

It should not diagnose diabetes, prescribe treatment, replace a doctor, or make emergency decisions.

If the knowledge base does not contain enough information, the chatbot should say:
“I do not have enough verified information to answer this safely.”
